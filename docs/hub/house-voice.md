These rules bind every string a user can see: page copy, seeded content
blocks, empty states, error states, email copy, and AI output at the boundary.
The handoff copy is final and was written to this voice. Do not rewrite it to
sound more like a nonprofit.

## The rules

1. Plain, short, declarative. Explain the thing, do not perform it.
2. No em dashes. Contractions always.
3. Never describe the community by what it lacks. "At-risk," "underserved,"
   and "give back" do not appear anywhere.
4. Every number carries its trust level: verified, estimated, stated, or
   placeholder. Never fill a gap with a plausible figure. A blank is better
   than a guess.
5. No nonprofit development jargon anywhere a user can see. "Moves
   management" and "cultivation" do not appear on any surface. (Source
   material such as the donor communication plan uses these words; they are
   translated at the boundary, never carried through.)
6. No emoji, no icons. Typographic marks only: the gold middle dot, mono
   labels, gold rules.
7. Growth is described, never scored. The audience is one person who is not
   a fundraiser; every big number explains itself in plain language.

## Why the voice is strict here

The hub's audience is Kendra, an Area Director doing fundraising work who is
not a development officer and should not have to be one. Copy that performs
expertise at her is copy that makes the page harder to open. The voice is the
opposite posture: the page explains itself, says what it knows and how well
it knows it, and renders what it does not know as a gap.
